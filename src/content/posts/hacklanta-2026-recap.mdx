The pitch was simple. One room, one night, the house always wins, and everyone who walked through the door was betting on themselves. It was the first hackathon progsu has ever hosted, and it became the biggest student hackathon GSU has seen.

![a packed lecture hall at hacklanta](/images/hacklanta/hacklanta-crowd.jpg)

## the floor

We went full casino night and the energy matched. Poker tables, felt, chips, and the kind of low hum a room only gets when a few hundred people are heads-down at 2am trying to make something work.

![poker table on the casino floor](/images/hacklanta/hacklanta-poker.jpg)

![the crowd at hacklanta, phones up](/images/hacklanta/hacklanta-floor-crowd.jpg)

<p class="pull">Different rhythm. Higher stakes.<cite>Hacklanta Spring 2026</cite></p>

Teams shipped everything from fintech tooling to AI-powered apps to a few things we genuinely didn't see coming. Mentors floated between tables. Someone always had a whiteboard going. The doughnuts never lasted long.

![an organizer in a hacklanta tee addressing the full room](/images/hacklanta/hacklanta-organizer.jpg)

![the hacklanta staff team](/images/hacklanta/hacklanta-staff.jpg)
