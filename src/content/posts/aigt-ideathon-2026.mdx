## the format

Beginner-friendly. No coding required. Teams of one to four picked one of six AI research areas, took the underlying paper or proposal, and built a startup concept on top of it. Then they pitched it in 1-2 minutes to a panel of judges.

Judging was simple:

- **Innovation**: Is the idea sharp?
- **Feasibility**: Could you actually build this?
- **Impact**: Does it matter?

Held at Tech Square in partnership with AIGT (AI at Georgia Tech).

## the research areas

These were the actual research papers and proposals teams worked off of. Not invented prompts. Real ongoing work from the AIGT lab:

- **Embodied agents**: self-evolving household robots that acquire skills over time
- **Vision-Language Action models (VLA)**: robotic arms that build and maintain a habitat from natural-language instructions
- **LLM-RewardSmith**: using LLMs to dynamically rewrite RL reward functions for terrain-adaptive robotic locomotion
- **LLM → SLM distillation**: decomposing big agents into specialized small language models for edge deployment
- **Clash Royale game AI**: autonomous agents that play Clash Royale via computer vision and imitation learning
- **Red-teaming**: adversarial testing of AI systems

The point: take a real piece of frontier research and answer the question "who would actually pay for this and why."
